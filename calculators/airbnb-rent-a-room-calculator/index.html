How it works, in plain English

Rent a Room Scheme

If you let furnished accommodation in the home you live in, the first £7,500 a year is tax-free. If someone else also receives letting income from the same home, each of you gets £3,750. Under the limit, there is nothing to report. Over it, you can either pay tax on everything above £7,500 (no expenses) or pay tax on your actual profit — whichever is lower. It works for lodgers and for short-stay guests through Airbnb or similar sites, as long as it is your main home while they stay.

The £1,000 property allowance

Anyone with property income can instead deduct a flat £1,000 rather than their real costs. It is handy when costs are very low, but it cannot be combined with Rent a Room on the same income.

Actual expenses

The ordinary route: income minus allowable costs such as platform commission, cleaning, laundry, consumables, insurance, a share of utility bills and repairs. For a separate holiday let with real running costs this usually wins. Mortgage interest on a separate let is relieved as a 20% credit rather than a deduction — use the landlord calculator if you have a mortgage on the property.

Holiday lets after April 2025

The furnished holiday lettings regime was abolished from 6 April 2025. Holiday lets are now taxed like any other rental property: no special capital allowances, no pension-earnings treatment and mortgage interest gets only the basic-rate credit.

Frequently asked questions

Can I use Rent a Room for Airbnb?

Yes, as long as guests stay in furnished accommodation in your own main home while you live there. It does not apply to a property you do not live in, such as a separate holiday cottage.

Is Airbnb income under £7,500 tax-free?

If it is from letting part of your own home under the Rent a Room Scheme, yes — and you do not need to report it. If it comes from a separate property, only the £1,000 property allowance is available, so anything over £1,000 is taxable.

What if my partner and I both get the rent?

Then the Rent a Room limit is split: £3,750 each. Tick the box in the calculator and enter your own share of the income and expenses.

Does Airbnb tell HMRC about my bookings?

Platforms now send HMRC a yearly report for hosts with 30 or more bookings or about £1,700 or more of income in a calendar year. That is not a tax, but HMRC can use it to check returns.

Do holiday lets still get special tax treatment?

No. The furnished holiday lettings rules ended on 5 April 2025. Holiday lets are now taxed as normal property income, and from April 2027 property income in England and Northern Ireland moves to 22%, 42% and 47% rates.
