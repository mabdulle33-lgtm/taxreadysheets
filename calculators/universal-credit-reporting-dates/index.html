How it works, in plain English

Universal Credit runs on monthly assessment periods. Each one starts on the same day of the month as your claim and ends the day before that date in the following month. What you earned in a period affects the payment made for it.

What to report

Self-employed claimants report their actual business receipts and expenses for the period, on a cash basis: money that came in and went out during those dates, not invoices raised or owed. You also report any tax and National Insurance you paid in that period. Universal Credit then works out your earnings for the month.

The reporting window

The calculator shows a window from 7 days before your period ends to 14 days after. Reporting early in the window means your payment can be calculated on time; leaving it late can delay or reduce a payment, so check your journal for the exact to-do date you are given. Payment usually arrives about 7 days after the period ends.

Start days of 29, 30 and 31

If your period starts on the 29th, 30th or 31st, it starts on the last day of the month in any month that does not have that date. For a start day of the 31st, that means 30 April, 30 June and 28 February (29 February in a leap year). The period before then ends one day earlier, so some periods are a few days shorter or longer than usual.

The minimum income floor

After any start-up period, Universal Credit may assume you earn at least a set amount each month (the minimum income floor) even if your real earnings are lower. That affects how much you get, but you still need to report your real figures every month.

Frequently asked questions

When do I report self-employed earnings for Universal Credit?

Each month, around the end of your assessment period. This calculator shows a window from 7 days before the period ends to 14 days after. Your online journal will show a to-do for each report — follow its date if it differs.

How do I find my assessment period start date?

Look at your Universal Credit statement in your online account. It shows the dates of each assessment period; the start date is the day after the previous one ended, and usually matches the date you first claimed.

My period starts on the 31st. What happens in shorter months?

In months with fewer than 31 days, the period starts on the last day of the month instead — for example 30 April or 28 February. The calculator handles this automatically for start days of 29, 30 and 31.

Do I report money I invoiced or money I received?

Money actually received and paid out during the assessment period. An invoice that is paid next month counts in next month's report.

Does Universal Credit reporting replace my tax return?

No. The monthly UC report is separate from HMRC. You still need to file a Self Assessment tax return for the tax year if you are self-employed, and keep records that support both.
