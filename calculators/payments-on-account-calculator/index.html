How it works, in plain English

On 31 January 2027 you file your 2025/26 return and pay what is left of the 2025/26 bill (the balancing payment). If you have to make payments on account, you also pay the first half of an advance towards 2026/27 on the same day, and the second half on 31 July 2027.

Who has to make payments on account

Everyone in Self Assessment does, unless last year's bill was less than £1,000, or more than 80% of your tax was taken at source, for example through PAYE on a job. Each payment is half of the income tax and Class 4 National Insurance on your last return. Student loan, Class 2 and capital gains tax are not included.

If your income has dropped

You can ask HMRC to reduce them: sign in to your online account, view your latest return and choose “Reduce payments on account”, or send form SA303. Only do this if you are confident, as HMRC charges interest if the real bill turns out higher.

If you cannot pay it all

If you owe less than £3,000, already pay tax through PAYE and file online by 30 December 2026, HMRC can collect the balance through your tax code instead. Otherwise, once you have filed, you can set up a payment plan online for bills up to £30,000. File on time either way: missing the deadline adds a £100 penalty even if you owe nothing.

If you pay late

For the 2025/26 bill, HMRC adds 5% of the balancing payment still unpaid after 30 days, and again at 6 and 12 months. Interest at 7.75% a year (Bank of England base rate plus 4%) runs on everything paid late, including payments on account. Full details are on GOV.UK.

Frequently asked questions

Why is my first January tax bill so big?

In your first year of Self Assessment, HMRC asks for the whole year's bill plus a first payment on account towards the next year, which is half as much again. So a £4,000 bill becomes £6,000 on 31 January and another £2,000 on 31 July. From the second year the payments on account you have already made come off the bill, so it evens out.

Do I have to make payments on account?

Yes, unless your last Self Assessment bill was less than £1,000, or more than 80% of your tax was already taken at source, for example through PAYE on a job or pension. Each payment is half of the income tax and Class 4 National Insurance on your last return.

What is not included in payments on account?

Student loan and postgraduate loan repayments, voluntary Class 2 National Insurance and capital gains tax are paid with your January bill but are not used to work out payments on account. Put them in the 'other amounts' box so the January total is right.

Can I reduce my payments on account?

Yes, if you expect your 2026/27 bill to be lower, for example because your income has dropped. Sign in to your HMRC online account, view your latest return and choose 'Reduce payments on account', or send form SA303. If you reduce them too far you pay interest on the shortfall.

What happens if I cannot pay by 31 January 2027?

File your return on time anyway, so you avoid the £100 late filing penalty. If you owe £30,000 or less you can set up a payment plan online after filing. A plan agreed before the 30-day point stops the 5% late payment penalty, but interest is still charged.

Do late payment penalties apply to payments on account?

For the 2025/26 bill, the 5% late payment penalties are charged on the balancing payment, not on payments on account. Interest, currently 7.75% a year, is charged on any late amount, including payments on account.
