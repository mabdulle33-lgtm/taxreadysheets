Budget 2026: what changes for the self-employed

The Autumn Budget on 28 October 2026 left income tax and National Insurance rates alone, but it did change a few things that matter to sole traders, side hustlers and landlords. Almost all of them start on 6 April 2027. Here is what changes, what does not, and what to do now.

The short version

  • No change for 2026/27. Income tax rates, Class 4 National Insurance (6% and 2%) and the £12,570 personal allowance stay the same. The bands remain frozen until April 2031.
  • Trading allowance triples from £1,000 to £3,000 from 6 April 2027.
  • Mileage: the 55p rate for the first 10,000 business miles is here to stay, and the rate after 10,000 miles rises from 25p to 35p from 6 April 2027.
  • Landlords pay more: rental profit will be taxed at 24%, 44% and 49% from 6 April 2027.
  • Making Tax Digital carries on as planned: income over £30,000 from April 2027 and over £20,000 from April 2028.

The trading allowance rises to £3,000

The trading allowance lets you earn a small amount from self-employment or side work tax-free, or deduct a flat amount instead of your real costs. From 6 April 2027 it goes up from £1,000 to £3,000. The government says people whose trading income is fully covered by the allowance will not need to declare it.

For the current 2026/27 tax year the limit is still £1,000. If your sales or side income go over £1,000 before 5 April 2027, you still need to register for Self Assessment by 5 October 2027. There is no matching rise for the £1,000 property allowance.

Mileage: 55p stays, 35p after 10,000 miles

HMRC raised the approved mileage rate for cars and vans from 45p to 55p a mile for the first 10,000 business miles in May 2026, backdated to 6 April 2026. The Budget confirmed it is permanent. So for 2026/27, use 55p for your first 10,000 miles and 25p after that.

From 6 April 2027 the rate for every business mile over 10,000 rises from 25p to 35p. That helps drivers, couriers, cleaners, carers and tradespeople who cover a lot of miles. Motorcycles (24p) and bicycles (20p) are unchanged.

Work out your 2026/27 claim with the mileage claim calculator.

Landlords: higher tax on rental profit

From 6 April 2027 rental profit gets its own tax rates. The November 2025 Budget set them at 22%, 42% and 47%; this Budget adds two more points, so they will be 24% basic, 44% higher and 49% additional in England and Northern Ireland. The tax credit for mortgage interest rises to 24% to match. Scotland and Wales can set their own property rates.

For 2026/27, rent is still taxed at the normal 20%, 40% and 45% with a 20% mortgage interest credit. Compare the two years with the landlord tax calculator.

Other changes to know about

  • Parental pay for self-employed parents: from April 2029, eligible self-employed fathers, partners and adopters will be able to get two weeks of parental pay.
  • Paying your tax bill: the government confirmed it will change when Self Assessment tax is paid, starting from 2029. Details will follow. Nothing changes for the 31 January 2027 and 31 January 2028 payments.
  • Savings interest outside an ISA will be taxed at 24%, 44% and 49% from April 2027. The cash ISA limit stays at £20,000.

What to do now

  1. If you drive for work, check your 2026/27 mileage claim uses 55p, not 45p.
  2. If you have a small side income, keep records as normal: the £3,000 allowance only starts on 6 April 2027.
  3. If you are a landlord, check how much the 2027/28 rates add to your bill and plan for it.
  4. Keep putting money aside for your tax bill. Our free tax set-aside calculator works out how much.

Frequently asked questions

Does the Budget change my 2026/27 tax bill?

No. Income tax rates, Class 4 National Insurance and the personal allowance are unchanged for 2026/27. The one thing to check is mileage: if you drive for work, use 55p a mile for your first 10,000 business miles in 2026/27, not the old 45p.

When does the £3,000 trading allowance start?

On 6 April 2027, for the 2027/28 tax year. For 2026/27 (6 April 2026 to 5 April 2027) the allowance is still £1,000.

What are the new tax rates for landlords?

From 6 April 2027, rental profit in England and Northern Ireland is taxed at 24% (basic), 44% (higher) and 49% (additional), and the mortgage interest credit rises to 24%. Scotland and Wales can set their own property rates.
