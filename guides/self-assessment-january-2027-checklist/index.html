31 January 2027: your Self Assessment checklist

On 31 January 2027 you need to file your 2025/26 tax return and pay what you owe. Millions leave it to January, when HMRC is busiest and mistakes are easiest to make. Work through this checklist and you will have everything ready, use the right figures for 2025/26, and know exactly what to pay.

What is due on 31 January 2027

By 11:59pm on 31 January 2027 you need to:

  • File your 2025/26 return online. It covers 6 April 2025 to 5 April 2026. The paper deadline (31 October 2026) has passed.
  • Pay the rest of your 2025/26 tax (the balancing payment), including Class 4 National Insurance and any student loan repayments.
  • Pay your first payment on account for 2026/27, if your bill is £1,000 or more and less than 80% of your tax was taken at source.

If you registered for Self Assessment after 5 October 2026, HMRC gives you three months from the date on its letter to file, but the tax is still due on 31 January 2027.

What to gather before you start

  • Your login and UTR. Your Government Gateway or One Login details and your 10-digit Unique Taxpayer Reference. If you have lost them, sort it now: new codes come by post and can take days.
  • Your income for 2025/26. Every sale, invoice and payment from 6 April 2025 to 5 April 2026, including selling apps and platforms. Platforms such as Etsy, Vinted, eBay and Airbnb now report sellers to HMRC, so your figures should match theirs.
  • Your expenses, by type. Stock, tools, phone, insurance, software, advertising, travel. Keep receipts: HMRC can ask for them for years.
  • Your mileage log if you drive for work, and your use-of-home hours if you work from home.
  • Any job or pension: your P60 (or P45) and any P11D benefits.
  • Other income: bank and savings interest, dividends, rent, CIS statements if you are a subcontractor.
  • Things that change your bill: pension contributions, Gift Aid donations, Child Benefit if anyone in the house earns over £60,000, and your student loan plan type.

Use the 2025/26 figures, not this year's

The most common slip in January is using today's figures on last year's return. For 2025/26:

Watch the mileage rate. The 55p rate started on 6 April 2026, so it belongs on next year's return, not this one. Scotland has its own income tax bands; Class 4 NI is the same across the UK.

The payments on account surprise

If your 2025/26 bill is £1,000 or more, HMRC usually asks for two advance payments towards 2026/27, each half of this year's bill: one on 31 January 2027 and one on 31 July 2027. In your first year that means paying one and a half times your bill in January.

Example: a £4,000 bill for 2025/26 means £6,000 on 31 January 2027 (the £4,000 plus a £2,000 payment on account) and £2,000 on 31 July 2027. If your income has dropped, you can ask HMRC to reduce them.

If you cannot pay it all

  • File on time anyway. The £100 late filing penalty applies even if you cannot pay.
  • Owe less than £3,000 and have a job or pension? File online by 30 December 2026 and HMRC can collect it through your tax code over 2027/28 instead of in one go.
  • Pay in smaller amounts before the deadline. You can pay towards your bill early, for example monthly, through GOV.UK or the HMRC app, as long as it is cleared by 31 January.
  • Set up a payment plan. Once you have filed, you can spread a bill of up to £30,000 online. Agree it before the 30-day point to avoid the 5% late payment penalty. Interest still runs.
  • Use the right reference. Pay using your UTR followed by the letter K, so the money lands in the right place.

If you miss the deadline

The 2025/26 return is under the current penalty rules for most people:

If something serious stopped you, such as a hospital stay or a bereavement, you can appeal with a reasonable excuse. If you joined Making Tax Digital voluntarily for 2025/26, the new points-based penalties apply to you instead. They apply to everyone from the 2027/28 tax year.

After you file: get 2026/27 sorted

Once January is done, make next year easier. For 2026/27, use 55p a mile for your first 10,000 business miles. If your income was over £50,000, Making Tax Digital quarterly updates started in April 2026; over £30,000, they start in April 2027 (you still file the 2025/26 return the usual way). Frequently asked questions

Which tax year is due on 31 January 2027?

The 2025/26 tax year, which ran from 6 April 2025 to 5 April 2026. You file that return online and pay the tax for it by 31 January 2027, along with your first payment on account for 2026/27 if you have to make them.

Should I use 45p or 55p a mile on my 2025/26 return?

45p. The 55p rate for the first 10,000 business miles only applies from 6 April 2026, so it is for your 2026/27 return. For 2025/26 use 45p for the first 10,000 miles and 25p after that.

I registered late. When is my return due?

If you registered after 5 October 2026, HMRC gives you a filing deadline three months from the date on its letter or email. The tax itself is still due by 31 January 2027, so pay what you expect to owe by then to avoid interest and penalties.

What happens if I miss the deadline?

There is an automatic £100 penalty, even if you owe nothing, then more after 3, 6 and 12 months. Late tax also gets a 5% penalty after 30 days and interest. If something serious stopped you filing, you can appeal with a reasonable excuse.
