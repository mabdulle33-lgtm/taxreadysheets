Your 2025/26 tax return, trade by trade

The general rules are the same for every sole trader, but each trade has its own traps on the 2025/26 return: CIS deductions to claim back, the old 45p mileage rate, platform reports, childminder home costs. Find your trade below, check the points that apply, and file before 31 January 2027.

What is due on 31 January 2027

Whatever your trade, by 11:59pm on 31 January 2027 you need to file your 2025/26 return online (it covers 6 April 2025 to 5 April 2026), pay any tax still owed for that year and, if HMRC asks for them, make your first payment on account for 2026/27. You can file any time from now: if you are due a refund, the sooner you file the sooner it arrives. Our 31 January checklist covers what to gather, payments on account and what to do if you cannot pay.

Making Tax Digital: your 2025/26 return is filed the normal way. MTD only changes things from April 2026 onwards, and only if your self-employed and property income before expenses is over the limit: £50,000 in 2024/25 brings you in from April 2026, £30,000 in 2025/26 from April 2027, and £20,000 in 2026/27 from April 2028. CIS subcontractors

Your tax return is how you get back CIS tax taken off your pay. Most subcontractors are owed money, because the 20% is taken before any allowance or expenses.

  • Put your gross pay in as income, not the amount that reached your bank, then enter the total CIS deducted in its own box (box 38 on the short self-employment pages, box 81 on the full pages).
  • Collect every monthly statement from each contractor for 6 April 2025 to 5 April 2026. If one is missing, ask the contractor for it: you can only claim back deductions you can show.
  • Check materials: CIS should only be taken from the labour part of an invoice. If a contractor deducted 20% from materials too, query it.
  • Claim your costs: tools, workwear, van or 45p mileage, phone, insurance and training all reduce your profit and increase your refund.

Taxi, private hire and delivery drivers

  • Use 45p a mile for 2025/26, not 55p. The new rate only applies to miles from 6 April 2026.
  • Pick one method per vehicle: either the mileage rate, or actual costs (fuel, repairs, insurance, plus capital allowances on the car). Once you have claimed actual costs and capital allowances on a vehicle, you cannot switch that vehicle to the mileage rate.
  • Use your full fares as income and claim the app's commission or service fee as an expense, so your figures line up with the annual statement the platform gives you and reports to HMRC.
  • Don't forget the extras: licence and badge fees, private hire insurance, phone and data, car cleaning and parking.

Hair and beauty

  • Chair or room rent is a business cost, as are products, towels, PPE, insurance and booking-app fees.
  • Tips count as income, whether paid in cash, by card or through an app.
  • Training: courses that keep your existing skills up to date are allowable. A course for a brand-new skill or service usually is not.
  • Got a salon job as well? Add your P60 to the return, so your allowance and tax bands are used correctly across both.

Vinted, eBay and Depop sellers

  • Selling your own unwanted things is not taxed. Buying items to sell on for a profit, or making things to sell, is trading.
  • Platform reports are not tax bills. Sites report sellers with 30 or more sales, or about £1,700 or more, in a calendar year. Being reported does not mean you owe tax, but if you were trading, HMRC can compare their figures with your return.
  • Trading income of £1,000 or less in 2025/26 (before costs) is covered by the trading allowance, so you don't need to declare it.
  • Profit is sales minus costs: what you paid for the items you sold, platform fees, postage and packaging.

Self-employed on Universal Credit

  • Universal Credit itself does not go on your tax return. It is not taxable.
  • Don't copy your monthly UC figures across. UC looks at each monthly assessment period, while your return covers the whole year to 5 April 2026. Use your own income and expense records for the year.
  • The £1,000 trading allowance only applies to tax. Universal Credit does not recognise it, so small amounts of self-employed income still need reporting to the DWP each month.

Dog walkers and pet sitters

  • Claim 45p a mile for driving between clients, parks and the vet, or actual costs if you use a dedicated van.
  • Everyday costs add up: insurance, DBS checks, leads, poo bags, treats, first-aid courses and your booking or invoicing app.
  • Home boarding or day care? Your council licence fee is a business cost, and if you work at home for 25 hours or more a month you can use the working-from-home flat rate.

Childminders

  • For 2025/26 you can still use HMRC's childminder method (set out in its manual at BIM52751): a share of household bills based on the hours you work, an estimate for the food you give the children, and a 10% deduction for wear and tear.
  • All your fees count as income, including funded hours paid by the council and payments made through Tax-Free Childcare.
  • Next year may be different: from April 2026, childminders who are in Making Tax Digital have to use the standard rules for home costs instead. That affects 2026/27 records, not this return.

Frequently asked questions

Do I use 45p or 55p a mile on my 2025/26 return?

45p for the first 10,000 business miles and 25p after that. The 55p rate only applies to miles driven from 6 April 2026, so it belongs on your 2026/27 return.

Can I file my 2025/26 return now?

Yes. You could file it from 6 April 2026, and the deadline is 31 January 2027. If you are due a refund, for example as a CIS subcontractor, filing early means you get it sooner.

I got a message from Vinted about HMRC. Do I have to pay tax?

Not necessarily. Platforms report sellers with 30 or more sales or about £1,700 or more in a calendar year. Selling your own unwanted things is not taxed. You only need to declare it if you were trading, such as buying items to resell, and your trading income was over £1,000.

Does Universal Credit go on my tax return?

No. Universal Credit is not taxable, so it is not included. You report your self-employed income and expenses for the tax year as normal.
